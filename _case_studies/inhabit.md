## Summary

In 2022 I wanted to embark on a new project, one for me. I'd gotten really into the fitness tech space and lifting, and had started on the "75 Hard" mental toughness challenge. I'd found habit-tracking apps to generally be lacking in design and functionality, and so set out to create my own. I wanted this to not just be another habit tracker, but something personal and productivity-igniting. To that end, I created Inhabit - "the world's first social habit tracking app".

My designer, [Isis Mawdsley-Diaz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/isismawdi/), created an incredible design that I did my best to faithfully commit to code. As with [BrawlQuest]({{ '/work/brawlquest/' | relative_url }}), the app was entirely hand-coded, before I used AI tools. Inhabit allowed you to create habits, take daily progress photos and view your habit streaks with a beautiful user interface. A "discipline" tab offered daily motivational quotes and articles. But that wasn't the key thing.

The USP of Inhabit was the "clubs" feature: as a paying user, you could set up a club and invite your friends. Clubs had their own sets of daily habits, and a chat room. When users ticked off the habits, it'd be posted in the chat to be celebrated. It was about accountability, and embarking on ambitious challenges with friends.

The app was built using Flutter/Dart, with the backend powered entirely by Firebase with JavaScript cloud functions to make push notifications etc tick. It launched in 2022, and is still available today.

## The brief

Habit tracking apps are lame. They're functional but they aren't beautiful or satisfying to use. There is no sense of community. I viewed Inhabit as a platform built off the back of great functionality. My intention was to add podcasts to the app, more articles, some sort of community forum where you could browse and clone habit sets&#46;&#46;&#46; stuff to make it feel like you were part of something.

Strava is a fascinating app because its success can be attributed to how it creates a sense of community. It's great at tracking and analysing runs and cycles - probably the best in its field - but its actual important feature is the feed and your profile. Hevy is the best app for tracking gym workouts, and it also has these social features built in. Inhabit wanted to be the Strava of habit tracking.

## Results

It didn't fail, but it wasn't a commercial success. We peaked at 8 active subscriptions, and 3 clubs that weren't ones I created. I used it extensively, as did several of my friends, so by that measure it was a success. But it's hard to sell a habit tracking app because the market is so oversaturated with them.

## What I'd point to

If I did it again, I'd lean even heavier into the social features, alongside publishing a web-version so people can track and talk online. That's important. The stack itself (Flutter, Dart, Firebase, NoSQL) was spot on for what the app was, and I wouldn't change any of that.
